PageSift 0.80.10 Release Notes: An Adaptive Page Limit\n\n> **Release Version**: `v0.80.10`\n> **Release Date**: 2026-10-28\n> **License**: AGPL-3.0-only (Commercial licensing available)\n\n---\n\n## Overview\n\nA focused crawl (`crawl --query`) used to need a page limit, and the limit decided how much was\nfound: on 69 universities in five directions with `--limit 12`, 181 of 307 crawls\nstopped because the limit was used up, not because the site had nothing more, and the outside\ntester's University of Manchester library crawl filled all 30 pages of `--limit 30`. In 0.80.10\nthe crawl decides how many pages it needs: with `--adaptive` and no `--limit`, it keeps going\nwhile pages add new relevant content and stops when they stop doing so, or when nothing relevant\ncan be found; the limit is only a cap. The fourteenth round of outside testing also found\ndiscovery problems (subtrees the crawl never reached, foreign-language main sites taking the\nbudget); those are fixed as general rules.\n\nThree extraction problems from the AiSeek report are fixed as well: a press-release template\nthat gave a 500-character teaser instead of the body, news sidebars kept as body text, and\nrelative links in uploaded HTML that pointed to `file:///`.\n\n**Behaviour changes to know about when upgrading:**\n\n- `crawl --adaptive` without `--limit` is capped at 500 pages instead of 100. With `--limit`\n  nothing changes about the cap.\n- An adaptive crawl no longer stops the moment the query is covered; it stops when the pages\n  that follow add nothing new. Adaptive crawls therefore deliver more pages.\n- A new stop reason, `no_relevant_pages`.\n\n---\n\n## The adaptive page limit\n\n`crawl --query … --adaptive` no longer needs `--limit`. Without it the crawl is capped at 500\npages (the HTTP API's `limit` already was a cap); the number of pages comes from three rules:\n\n- **Covered and nothing new.** Reaching the coverage threshold no longer stops the crawl at\n  once: the Manchester library run reached it while one branch library after another was still\n  coming in. The crawl goes on while pages add new relevant content and stops after\n  `ceil(patience / 2)` pages in a row that add nothing, unless the best queued link still carries\n  a sign of the query. Coverage needs evidence from at least two pages; one overview page can\n  mention every query word often enough (a Tsinghua housing crawl used to stop on its 2nd page).\n- **Nothing new** (`saturated`): unchanged, `patience` pages in a row without new relevant\n  content, unless better links are still queued.\n- **Nothing to find** (`no_relevant_pages`, new): while nothing relevant has been found,\n  `2 × patience` pages in a row reached only through links without any sign of the query (a\n  score no higher than the section prior) stop the crawl. Such sites used to run to the limit:\n  at `--limit 100`, 17 of the 25 crawls that used the whole limit had no useful page at all. The\n  factor 2 was chosen by replaying 0.80.9's `--limit 100` results: it saves about a quarter of\n  the pages and loses under 5% of the useful ones, all on sites where the crawl wandered for dozens\n  of pages before stumbling on content.\n\nMeasured on the same 69 universities and five directions as 0.80.9 (international students,\ngraduate admission, housing, computer science, library; robots.txt ignored so every version sees\nthe same pages), 297 crawls that every run reached, graded by URL and title with 0.80.9's\nrules (U: squarely on the query, M: related):\n\n| Run | Pages | Median per site | U pages | U share | U+M share | Sites with no U |\n| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |\n| 0.80.9, `--limit 12` | 3,223 | 12 | 645 | 20.0% | 48.8% | 107 |\n| 0.80.9, `--limit 100` | 10,787 | 26 | 1,856 | 17.2% | 48.0% | 61 |\n| 0.80.10, adaptive, no limit | 16,351 | 48 | 4,208 | 25.7% | 66.1% | 52 |\n\nThe adaptive crawl reads more pages where there is more to read (the largest crawl took 321\npages, none reached the cap) and a larger share of what it reads is useful. Stop reasons:\n`coverage_reached` 192, `saturated` 72, `no_relevant_pages` 32, `exhausted` 1. Raw results and scripts: [`benchmarks/history/0.80.10/`](https://github.com/YOLOW92/pagesift/blob/v0.80.10/benchmarks/history/0.80.10).\n\n## Discovery fixes\n\n- **Foreign-language main sites.** For an English query, section links get a minimum score so\n  that portals are explored before news. On sites whose main language is not English this sent\n  the crawl into the German (TUM) or Norwegian (Oslo) main site, whose sections the query cannot\n  read. Once the site's English content is known (the start page is English, or the English home\n  page was found), links on pages in other languages no longer get that minimum; queued ones are\n  re-scored when the English home page is found. `/english/research/` now counts as a\n  first-level section of the English site, like `/en/research/`.\n- **Services under \"about\".** A short section named after a query word under an about or news\n  section (`/about/housing/`, `/news/international-students/`) is content. The University of\n  Washington's home page links \"Student housing\" to `/about/housing/`; it was demoted as an about\n  page and the housing crawl found no housing page in 12. Long article slugs under `/news/` stay\n  news.\n- The Manchester library case (the library subsite first reached around the 14th page) is\n  covered by the adaptive limit: the crawl no longer ends before it gets there.\n\n## Extraction fixes (AiSeek report)\n\n- **Teaser instead of the body.** Some press-release templates put the headline and a\n  truncated summary in their own `<article>` and the body outside it; the article that owns the\n  headline was taken as the body, so about 500 characters ending in \"...\" came out instead of\n  3,300 to 11,600. When that article is short and its text reappears in a longer block elsewhere\n  on the page, the longer block is the body and the headline is kept.\n- **Sidebars kept.** In two-column layouts without an article marker the body region stopped at\n  the container of both columns, so the other column's story list came out as body text. The\n  region (from either the container rules or the Readability-style scoring) is now narrowed\n  from the heading that matches `<title>` to the smallest container that still holds most of\n  the body text. Story cards (a link next to a short summary ending in an ellipsis) do not count\n  as body text. When the page's only `<h1>` is a section name and an `<h2>` matches `<title>`,\n  the `<h2>` is the headline; taking the section name used to widen the region to include the\n  sidebar.\n- **Relative links in uploads.** `POST /v2/parse` takes an optional `url` field, the address the\n  uploaded HTML came from. Relative links resolve against it (a `<base href>` in the page still\n  wins), and it becomes `metadata.sourceURL`. It is never fetched.
